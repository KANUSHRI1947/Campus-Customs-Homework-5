Reflection

Question 1

How would you evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket and why?

Ticket 101 was good for the agents, but at first, there was a huge issue in the workflow that first kept me from being able to approve resolving the ticket and then kept the ticket from being marked resolved. The plan ultimately became Inventory to Accounting to Inventory to Customer Service, which worked well. Ticket 102, on the other hand, moved very simply. It was just a rent issue, so the Boss just had to call Facilities so that the lease info could be verified and then give the financial part to Accounting. Lastly, for Ticket 103, the agents performed well, but only after the system took my decision to allow the discount. The plan was for Inventory to go first, but then it needed me (the human) to agree to the discount. Overall, the agents performed well after some hiccups.

Question 2

For each ticket, how did Actual compare with Expected?

For Ticket 101, Actual was close to Expected in terms of which agents we ended up using because I expected Inventory first, followed by Accounting, followed by Inventory again and then Customer Service. The difference was what definitions allowed for the ticket to be marked as ‘resolved.’ The need for restock was going to keep the ticket open forever, so Actual had to improve that definition. For Ticket 102, Actual was pretty much exactly like Expected. I expected Facilities first, followed by Accounting, which is what happened. For Ticket 103, the agents went as expected from Inventory to Customer Service, but the human-decision mechanism needed to be added to finish up the problem.

Question 3

What would have been simpler as one agent with tools and why?

Ticket 102 could have been easier as one agent with tools. It was a very simple path from Facilities to Accounting. If there were one agent with the lease/rent tool, that would have been easier as a workflow than the back-and-forth we ended up with.

Question 4

What are three new problems Campus Customs might face that this agent team could solve with the tools you built?

  1. It could figure out how to pay another bill besides rent, just like it paid vendor invoice 501 ($840) for Ticket 101 and the $2,400 lease rent for Ticket 102, with my approval each time.
  2. It could plan a partial fill of an order for a customer and let the person know what is in stock vs. what is still missing, like in Ticket 103, where Inventory found 8 of the 20 size M hoodies in stock and 12 still missing, and Customer Service drafted a reply to the Yale AI Club.
  3. It could tell a customer what is available vs. not, like in Ticket 101, where Customer Service drafted a reply telling Tauhid Zaman the size S tee was out of stock, with an expected arrival of 2026-09-05.

Question 5

What are three problems Campus Customs might face that this agent team could not solve?

  1. It would not be able to log that a restocking shipment had arrived and consequently increase the stock. In Ticket 101, the tee restock could only be saved as a draft with an expected arrival date; nothing can mark it received or raise the size S count from 0.
  2. It would not be able to give a customer a refund if they wanted to return their purchase. In Tickets 101 and 102, the payment tool could only pay a vendor invoice or the rent, so it has no way to pay a customer back or record a returned item.
  3. It would not be able to find a new vendor for inventory that someone wants but that we do not currently have. In Ticket 103, no vendor was linked to the hoodie in our data, so the team could not find anyone to restock the 12 missing hoodies.
